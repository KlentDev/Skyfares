Chapter 1 — How KrisFlyer Actually Works
Singapore Airlines’ KrisFlyer is widely regarded as one of the most powerful and aspirational frequent flyer programmes in the world. For the savvy Singapore-based traveller, it is the only mainstream programme that can consistently unlock a flat bed to Europe or a private suite above the clouds, all booked directly with miles.

This is a guide on the mechanics that separate a good redemption from a spectacular one. We will focus on the rules, the strategy, and the art of building a redemption so that you choose KrisFlyer with your eyes wide open.

What KrisFlyer Is
KrisFlyer is the frequent flyer programme of Singapore Airlines. It is also the primary portal to redeem miles on Star Alliance partners and a handful of other affiliated carriers. At its core, it is an award-booking engine, not a distance-based programme.

There is no hidden cash value to your mile. The programme is based on charts by zone and cabin, with costs subject to devaluations over time. The game for us in Singapore is to target redemptions where the cost of miles plus taxes is massively lower than the cash fare, and where the cash fare is simply unattainable.

The Three Award Types
KrisFlyer Award Types
1. Saver Awards
This is the cheapest award level and the one every member hunts for. Capacity is limited. When seats are gone, they are rarely replenished unless someone cancels.

2. Advantage Awards
This costs roughly 15-20% more than Saver, with greater seat availability. This is your lifeline when plans are fixed and Saver space has vanished. It is also the only award level that allows changes to date and flight time for a fee.

3. Access Awards
Introduced from November 2025, Access awards are tied directly to cash pricing. They are priced at up to 3x the Saver rate. This ensures a seat is almost always available for purchase with miles, but at a severe premium. Think of Access as a cap on value, not a redemption to target.

Key Rules
Saver is the sweet spot. Advantage is your Plan B. Access is to be avoided unless you have zero alternative.
You can waitlist for Saver seats even if Advantage is showing available.
Award availability for Singapore Airlines flights opens at 355 days before departure.
The Two Award Charts
This is the single biggest point of confusion for newcomers. KrisFlyer uses two entirely separate redemption tables.

1. Singapore Airlines / SilkAir / Scoot Chart
This is used only when you fly exclusively on Singapore Airlines Group metal. It is zone-based. For example, Singapore to London via Singapore is one price. Singapore to London via Frankfurt is the same price as long as all segments are on SQ.

2. Star Alliance Award Chart
This chart applies the moment a non-Singapore Airlines Group carrier enters your itinerary. For example, Singapore to London on Thai via Bangkok, or Singapore to Frankfurt on Lufthansa. The pricing is different, and often higher, than the SQ chart.

Zones, Not Distance
KrisFlyer zones are large and sometimes illogical. Tokyo, Seoul, and Beijing are all in the same North Asia zone. This means Singapore to Tokyo costs the same in miles as Singapore to Seoul, despite the extra distance. Similarly, London, Frankfurt, and Zurich are all in the same Europe zone.

This creates arbitrage opportunities. Flying to the furthest edge of a zone delivers the most value per mile.

The One-Way Rule
KrisFlyer redemptions are priced per segment. A round trip is simply two one-ways added together. This is powerful because it allows you to mix and match cabins and airlines for each direction. You can fly out to London in Business Class on Saver and return in First Class on Advantage if that is all that is available.

What to take from this chapter
Master Saver awards. Treat Advantage as your backup. Ignore Access.
Understand which chart you are booking on. Mixing SQ with a Star Alliance partner forces the Star Alliance chart for the entire journey.
Think in one-way segments and zones to maximise value.
Chapter 2 — Earning Miles in Singapore
Earning miles efficiently in Singapore is a science. With the right setup, a normal professional with regular spending can earn a Business Class return to Japan or Europe every single year without flying. The key is to understand the ecosystem.

The Two Paths
There are two main ways to earn KrisFlyer miles via credit cards in Singapore.

Path 1: Co-branded KrisFlyer Cards
These cards earn KrisFlyer miles directly into your account every month. The earn rates are generally higher for Singapore Airlines Group spending.

Card	General Spend Rate	SQ Group Spend Rate	Key Perk
American Express KrisFlyer Card	1.1 mpd	2 mpd	No min spend
American Express KrisFlyer Ascend	1.2 mpd	2 mpd	Free annual hotel night, 10,000-mile bonus
KrisFlyer UOB Credit Card	1.2 mpd	3 mpd	Fast track to KrisFlyer Elite Silver
Path 2: General Miles Cards
These cards earn bank points, which you then transfer to KrisFlyer. This is superior for flexibility because points do not expire as long as they are sitting in your bank account. You only transfer when you need to book.

Card	Best Earn Rate	Key Categories
Citi PremierMiles Card	2 mpd	Foreign currency spend
DBS Altitude Card	2.2 mpd	Online flight and hotel bookings, foreign currency
UOB PRVI Miles Card	2.4 mpd	Foreign currency, travel bookings
HSBC Revolution Card	4 mpd	Online spend, contactless, capped at $1,000/month
Standard Chartered Journey Card	2 mpd	Local and overseas dining, transport
The Welcome Bonus Engine
The single fastest way to accumulate miles is through credit card welcome bonuses. Banks in Singapore frequently offer bonuses of 20,000 to 45,000 miles for new-to-bank customers who hit a minimum spend, usually S$1,000 to S$3,000 in the first one to three months.

A couple cycling through cards strategically can earn over 150,000 miles per year just from welcome bonuses. This alone is enough for a return Business Class trip to Japan.

The 36-Month Rule
Miles earned directly into KrisFlyer, from flying or co-branded cards, expire 36 months after the month they are credited. They are hard-expiry. You cannot extend them.

Miles earned via bank points do not expire until you transfer them. This is why Path 2 is superior for most people. You keep your miles safe in the bank ecosystem and only transfer them to KrisFlyer when you have a redemption ready to book.

Earning Beyond Credit Cards
Kris+: The Kris+ app allows you to earn up to 9 mpd at participating merchants in Singapore by paying via the app. You can then transfer these miles to KrisFlyer instantly.
KrisShop: Earn miles on duty-free and merchandise purchases. Promotions often run at 5 to 15 mpd.
Pelago: The SIA-owned travel experiences platform earns miles on activities and tours booked worldwide.
Hotel Partners: Most major hotel chains allow you to convert points to KrisFlyer, though the ratios are generally poor. The best play here is during transfer bonus promotions, which occur a few times a year.
What to take from this chapter
Use general miles cards for flexibility. Keep points in the bank until needed.
Cycle welcome bonuses as a couple to generate 100,000+ miles per year with minimal spend.
Remember that KrisFlyer miles expire in 36 months. Never hoard miles in your KrisFlyer account.
Leverage Kris+, KrisShop, and Pelago for bonus miles on spending you were doing anyway.
Chapter 3 — Spontaneous Escapes: The Monthly Window Most Members Miss
Spontaneous Escapes is a monthly promotion where Singapore Airlines releases seats at a 30% discount off the usual Saver rate for travel in the following month. It is the closest thing to a flash sale in the miles world.

The Mechanics
Around the middle of each month, usually the 15th, Singapore Airlines publishes a list of destinations on its website. Bookings open for a short period, typically two weeks. Travel must be completed within the next calendar month.

The discount applies to Saver Business, Premium Economy, and Economy. It never applies to Suites, First Class, or Advantage awards.

How It Works
You book Spontaneous Escapes directly on the Singapore Airlines website like a normal award. The discounted rate is automatically applied to eligible routes and dates. Availability is limited and driven by unsold seats. Popular routes vanish within hours of release.

Recurring Sweet Spots
While the list changes monthly, certain routes appear frequently and represent outstanding value.

Tokyo, Osaka, Seoul: Regularly appears in Business Class at 40,600 miles one-way instead of 58,000. This is the best Business Class sweet spot in the entire programme.
Beijing, Shanghai: Often available at 28,700 miles in Business instead of 41,000.
South Asia: Maldives, Sri Lanka, and India frequently appear. Colombo at 18,200 miles in Business instead of 26,000 is superb value.
Johannesburg: When it appears, it is arguably the best deal in the network at 47,600 miles in Business instead of 68,000 for a 10-hour flight.
Europe: Appears less often, but when Frankfurt, Zurich, or London shows up, it is typically 66,500 miles in Business instead of 95,000. This is a saving of 57,000 miles for a round trip.
JFK to Frankfurt: The fifth freedom route occasionally appears at 47,600 miles in Business instead of 68,000.
How to Read the List Strategically
Do not look at the list and think, "Where can I go next month?" Look at it and ask, "Can I build a bigger trip around this?"

For example, if Sydney appears at 35,700 miles in Business instead of 51,000, you could book that and then buy a cheap cash ticket from Sydney to New Zealand or Melbourne to create a longer holiday.

The best strategy is to keep your calendar relatively free for the next month and pounce when a high-value route appears.

The Scoot Version
Scoot also runs its own Spontaneous Escapes with 25% off ScootPlus and Economy. This is less aspirational but valuable for short regional hops to Thailand, Malaysia, or Indonesia where cash fares are low and taxes on a full KrisFlyer redemption make it poor value.

Common Mistakes
Booking too late: If you see something you want, book immediately. Waiting until the next day means it will likely be gone.
Ignoring taxes: Taxes on a Spontaneous Escapes award to London are still several hundred dollars. Calculate the total cost before assuming it is a steal.
Forcing a bad trip: Do not fly to a destination you have no interest in just because it is cheap. You will spend more on hotels and activities than you saved on miles.
What to take from this chapter
Spontaneous Escapes offers 30% off Saver awards for last-minute travel.
The best value is on long-haul Business Class to Japan, South Africa, and Europe.
Book immediately upon release. Availability disappears fast.
Always calculate taxes to ensure the total cost makes sense.
Chapter 4 — The Sweet Spots: Where KrisFlyer Beats Every Other Programme
Not all redemptions are created equal. The art of KrisFlyer is to find routes where the miles required are low relative to the cash price and the quality of the cabin. These are the sweet spots.

Short-Haul Asia
China and South Asia
Bangalore, Chennai, Delhi, Mumbai in Business Class for 26,000 miles one-way is excellent value. The flight is around 5 hours with full flat-bed service. Colombo at 26,000 miles is equally strong.

Hong Kong, Taipei, Bangkok
These are priced at 20,500 miles in Business one-way. While the flight time is only 3 to 4 hours, the value is solid if cash fares are high. Do not redeem for Economy on these routes as taxes make it poor value.

Aspirational Long-Haul Europe
Business Class to Europe
Any European city in Business for 95,000 miles one-way Saver is the core sweet spot for most Singaporeans. This applies to London, Frankfurt, Zurich, Paris, and more. A round trip is 190,000 miles plus taxes of around S$400 to S$800 depending on the city.

First Class and Suites to Europe
This is the pinnacle. Frankfurt, London, and Zurich are served by the A380 with Suites, or the 777-300ER with First Class. The cost is 132,000 miles one-way Saver. Availability is the main hurdle, which we will cover in Chapter 5.

The Frankfurt Routing Trick
Frankfurt is often easier to find in First or Suites than London. You can book Singapore to Frankfurt in Suites, then connect onward to London on Lufthansa in Business using the Star Alliance chart for the second segment, or buy a cheap cash ticket. This avoids the UK APD tax, which we will cover in Chapter 6.

Fifth Freedom Routes
These are routes where Singapore Airlines operates between two foreign countries. They are often better value because demand is lower from the non-Singapore point.

JFK to Frankfurt: 68,000 miles in Business, 96,000 miles in First. This is a 7-hour flight and one of the cheapest ways to experience Singapore Airlines First Class.
Los Angeles to Tokyo Narita: 86,500 miles in Business, 122,000 miles in First. This is an 11-hour flight and excellent value compared to US airlines.
Star Alliance Partner Plays
When SQ has no availability, partners can save your trip, often at a lower mileage cost on the Star Alliance chart.

Thai Airways: Extensive network into Europe via Bangkok. Business Class is solid, and availability is generally better than SQ.
Air India: Avoid. The product is inconsistent and the service is not to Singapore Airlines standards.
EVA Air: Superb Business Class via Taipei to North America. The 787 and 777 products are excellent.
Turkish Airlines: Strong Business Class via Istanbul. The catering is legendary. Good for reaching secondary European cities.
United: Use only as a last resort. The product is below par for the mileage cost.
Australia and South Pacific
Australia in Business Class costs 51,000 miles one-way to the east coast and 58,000 miles to Perth. This is decent value for a 5 to 7 hour flight. New Zealand is 68,000 miles, which is weaker value due to the longer distance but still cheaper than cash.

Routes to AVOID
UK Departures in Premium Cabins: The UK Air Passenger Duty makes taxes on Business and First exorbitant. We will detail this in Chapter 6.
Lufthansa First Class from Frankfurt: Bookable only within 15 days of departure for partners. Not practical for planning.
Routes with Low Cash Fares: Redeeming for Economy to Kuala Lumpur or Bangkok rarely makes sense. You will pay S$100+ in taxes for a ticket that costs S$150 in cash.
The Star Alliance Chart vs SQ Chart Decision
Always price out both options. As a rule, if your entire itinerary is on Singapore Airlines, use the SQ chart. If you must include a partner, the whole journey prices on the Star Alliance chart, which can be more or less expensive depending on the zone. For example, Singapore to New York via Frankfurt is cheaper on SQ metal than via Taipei on EVA.

What to take from this chapter
The best value is in Business and First Class to Japan, South Africa, Europe, and on Fifth Freedom routes.
Frankfurt is your gateway to First and Suites if London is unavailable.
Use Star Alliance partners to fill gaps, but vet the product quality.
Never redeem for short-haul Economy where taxes exceed 50% of a cash fare.
Chapter 5 — Suites and First Class: The 355-Day Strategy
Securing a seat in Singapore Airlines Suites or First Class is the ultimate KrisFlyer game. It requires planning, speed, and an understanding of how SQ releases seats.

Suites vs First Class
On the A380, the product is called Suites. It is a private room with a separate bed and chair. On the 777-300ER, it is called First Class. It is a large open suite with a seat that converts to a bed. Both cost the same in miles. Suites is newer and more private, but First Class on the 777 offers fantastic service and is often easier to find.

The 355-Day Rule
Singapore Airlines loads its schedule and award inventory 355 days before departure. At approximately 8:00 AM Singapore time, 355 days out, Saver space in First and Suites is released for PPS Club members only. At 8:00 AM the next day, 354 days out, that space becomes available to all KrisFlyer members.

For popular routes like Singapore to London or Zurich, Saver First Class space can disappear within minutes of becoming available to the public.

The Mechanics
Use a calendar to calculate 355 days before your desired departure date.
Log in to your KrisFlyer account at 7:55 AM Singapore time on that day.
Have your route and dates pre-loaded in the search engine.
At 8:00 AM sharp, refresh and search. If you are not PPS, check again at 8:00 AM the next day for general member release.
Have your miles ready in your account. Transfers from banks are instant but do not risk it. Transfer the day before.
When to Book Advantage or Access
If you miss the Saver release, you have two choices.

Advantage: Costs 15-20% more but allows you to lock in a seat. This is acceptable if the trip is non-negotiable, for example a honeymoon or anniversary. You can set a waitlist for Saver and downgrade if it clears.

Access: Avoid. The markup is punitive. You would be burning 300,000+ miles for a one-way Suites ticket that costs 132,000 on Saver. Book a cash ticket or fly Business instead.

The Long Game
If you cannot find availability at 355 days, do not give up.

Set Alerts: Use ExpertFlyer or manually check daily. Airlines release unsold revenue seats as awards as the departure date approaches.
Check at T-14 and T-7 days: Singapore Airlines often releases additional First and Suites space 14 days and 7 days before departure.
Be Flexible: Search for single seats. Finding two Saver First seats together at 355 days is extremely rare. Consider splitting up and one of you flying Business.
Consider Fifth Freedoms: The JFK-FRA and LAX-NRT routes have better First Class availability than flights out of Singapore.
What to take from this chapter
To book Suites or First, you must be ready at 355 days out, 8:00 AM Singapore time.
Saver is the only logical award type. Advantage is Plan B. Never use Access.
If you miss the initial release, monitor for space at T-14 and T-7 days.
Be flexible on dates and consider flying solo or via Fifth Freedom routes.
Chapter 6 — The Hidden Levers: Taxes, Stopovers, and the UK APD Trap
These are the advanced rules and quirks that can save you thousands of dollars in taxes or unlock trips that seem impossible.

The UK Air Passenger Duty Trap
The UK government levies an Air Passenger Duty (APD) on all flights departing the UK. For long-haul flights in Business and First, this tax is severe.

From April 2026, the APD rates are:

Business Class: £216 per passenger
First Class: £236 per passenger
This is charged on top of airport taxes and fuel surcharges. A one-way First Class award from London to Singapore can incur over S$800 in taxes and fees, with APD being the largest component.

How to Avoid It
Do not start your journey in London. Fly into London, but depart from Paris, Amsterdam, or Frankfurt on your return. The train to Paris is 2 hours and 15 minutes.
Position to Europe first. Book Singapore to Frankfurt in Suites, spend a few days there, then take a cheap cash flight to London for your main trip. Return from Frankfurt to Singapore in Suites.
When NOT to Position
If your trip is short and only to London, the cost and time of positioning may outweigh the APD saving. Run the numbers. For a family of four, the saving is over S$1,500, which justifies an extra flight. For a solo traveller with limited time, it may not.

Stopovers
KrisFlyer has generous stopover rules that can turn one trip into two.

Saver Awards: One complimentary stopover is allowed on a round-trip award. On a one-way award, no stopover is allowed.
Advantage Awards: Two complimentary stopovers are allowed on a round-trip, or one on a one-way.
Cost: An additional stopover can be purchased for USD 100.
How to Use Stopovers Strategically
Book Singapore to London. Add a stopover in Frankfurt on the way there for no extra miles. Spend 3 days in Frankfurt, then continue to London. On the way back, fly direct London to Singapore.

This costs the same in miles as Singapore to London direct, but gives you a second city for free.

The Stopover Trick That No Longer Works
In the past, you could book Singapore to Bangkok to Tokyo, with a stopover in Bangkok, for the price of Singapore to Tokyo. This loophole was closed. Your origin, destination, and stopover point must now make geographical sense. You cannot backtrack.

Holding Awards
Singapore Airlines no longer allows you to hold an award ticket for free while you transfer miles. You must have the full mileage balance in your account at the time of booking.

The workaround is to transfer miles speculatively before availability appears if you are chasing a 355-day release. If you fail to secure the seat, the miles are stuck in KrisFlyer with a 36-month expiry. This is a risk you must weigh.

Mixing Cabins
You can mix Saver and Advantage on a single one-way booking. For example, Singapore to Frankfurt in Business Saver, connecting to London in Business Advantage if Saver is not available on the short haul. The system will price it proportionally. This is useful for securing the long-haul segment you want.

Fuel Surcharge Awareness
Singapore Airlines no longer imposes fuel surcharges on its own flights. However, they still apply on partner airlines. A Thai Airways flight will carry a fuel surcharge, making the taxes higher than on an SQ flight. Always check the full tax breakdown before confirming.

Spontaneous Escapes Hold Window
Spontaneous Escapes awards are non-changeable and non-refundable. You cannot pay a fee to change the date. If your plans are uncertain, do not book Spontaneous Escapes. Book a regular Saver award that allows changes for a USD 25 fee.

Key Rules
Never start a premium cabin redemption in London. The APD is punitive.
Use the complimentary stopover on Saver round-trips to add a free city.
You cannot hold awards. Have miles in your account before you search.
Mixing Saver and Advantage cabins is allowed and often necessary.
Chapter 7 — Protecting Your Miles and Playing the Long Game
Earning miles is only half the game. Protecting them from expiry and devaluation is the other half. A mile earned has zero value until it is redeemed for a flight you would have paid cash for.

The Cardinal Rule
Never let miles expire. KrisFlyer miles expire 36 months after the month they are earned. There is no way to extend them. If you have a large balance and no travel plans, redeem them for something, even if it is suboptimal, rather than let them die.

The 36-Month Expiry Clock
The clock starts ticking the moment miles hit your KrisFlyer account. It does not pause. It does not reset. If you earned 50,000 miles in August 2023, they will expire on 31 August 2026.

This is why you should not transfer bank points to KrisFlyer until you are ready to book. A bank point transferred in August 2026 expires in August 2029, even if you earned that point in 2023.

The Build-to-Burn Cycle
Do not hoard miles. Devaluations happen every two to three years. A Business Class seat to Europe that costs 95,000 miles today may cost 110,000 miles after the next devaluation.

The correct strategy is a continuous build-to-burn cycle. As a household, you should aim to earn 200,000 to 300,000 miles per year and burn a similar amount. Your KrisFlyer balance should rarely exceed what you need for your next two major trips.

Keep Miles in Bank Programmes
The banks are your friends here. DBS Points, Citi Miles, UOB UNI$, and HSBC Rewards Points do not expire as long as your card is active. This gives you unlimited storage time. You can build a war chest of 500,000 miles across banks and sleep easy knowing they are safe.

Only transfer to KrisFlyer when you have a specific redemption in mind and have confirmed award space is available.

Surviving a Devaluation
Singapore Airlines typically announces devaluations with one to two months’ notice. When an announcement hits, you have a final window to book at the old rates.

Your action plan:

Immediately transfer all bank points to KrisFlyer.
Book speculative trips for the next 11 months. You can book 355 days out.
Lock in First and Suites redemptions as they are hit hardest by devaluations.
Do not panic-book bad redemptions. A bad redemption is still a loss, even at old rates.
A Final Word
KrisFlyer is a tool. It is not a bank account. The goal is to convert your everyday spending into experiences that would be irrational to pay for in cash. A flat bed to New York. A private suite to London. A spontaneous weekend in Tokyo.

Master the rules. Respect the expiry clock. Stay ready at 355 days. And never, ever pay Access rates.

This is the game. Play it well.

What to take from this chapter
KrisFlyer miles expire in 36 months. Never forget this.
Keep your miles in bank programmes until you need to book.
Operate a build-to-burn cycle. Do not hoard miles through devaluations.
When a devaluation is announced, transfer and book immediately.
The game is about experiences, not mile balances.
